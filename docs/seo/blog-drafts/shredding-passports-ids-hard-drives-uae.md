## AEO Answer Block

**Can you shred old passports, ID documents, and hard drives in the UAE?**
Old passport copies and Emirates ID copies made of standard paper can be shredded on a cross-cut (DIN P-4 or higher) office shredder like any confidential document. Original government-issued ID booklets often have a plastic/laminated data page that standard paper shredders aren't rated for — these need a shredder specifically rated for card/laminate destruction, or a specialist service. Hard drives and other digital media require physical destruction (shredding, crushing, or degaussing) rather than a paper shredder, since deleting files doesn't remove the underlying data.

## Introduction

Some of the most sensitive items in an office aren't loose paper — they're identity documents and old electronic media. Both need different handling than a standard confidential-paper shredding routine.

---

## Passport & ID Copies (Paper)

Photocopies of passports and Emirates IDs — the kind kept in HR files, visa applications, or client onboarding records — are standard paper and can go through a regular cross-cut office shredder at DIN P-4 or higher, the same as any other confidential document.

## Original ID Booklets & Cards (Laminated/Plastic)

The physical passport booklet or a plastic Emirates ID card is a different problem:
- **Laminated data pages** can jam or damage a standard paper shredder not rated for lamination
- **Plastic ID cards** need a shredder specifically rated for card destruction, not a paper-only unit

If your office needs to destroy expired original ID cards (e.g. old company access cards, expired visa cards), ask about a card-rated shredder rather than feeding them into a standard paper unit.

---

## Hard Drives & Digital Media

**Deleting a file, or even reformatting a drive, does not destroy the underlying data** — it can often be recovered. Secure destruction of hard drives and digital media requires:

- **Physical shredding** — industrial media shredders cut drives into small fragments
- **Crushing/punching** — physically deforms the platters beyond data recovery
- **Degaussing** — erases magnetic storage using a strong magnetic field (not effective on solid-state drives)

For most UAE offices, an occasional hard drive or old server needing destruction is best handled through a specialist media destruction service rather than attempting it with paper-shredding equipment, which isn't built for metal and electronic components.

## Why This Matters: What's Actually on These Items

| Item | What It Exposes If Not Destroyed |
|---|---|
| Passport/ID copy | Full name, ID number, date of birth, nationality — enough for identity theft |
| Original ID card | Same, plus a physical credential that could be used for impersonation |
| Hard drive | Potentially everything ever stored on it — emails, financial records, client data, credentials |

A single unshredded ID copy or a discarded hard drive can expose far more personal information than a stack of paper documents, which is why these need deliberate, specific handling rather than being thrown in with general office waste.

## FAQ

**Q: Can I shred a passport photocopy on my rented office shredder?**
A: Yes, standard paper photocopies of passports and IDs go through a regular cross-cut shredder like any confidential document.

**Q: How much does hard drive shredding cost?**
A: Pricing varies by volume and method (shredding vs crushing vs degaussing) — for occasional single-drive destruction, ask a specialist media destruction provider for a quote; volume matters more than complexity for pricing.

**Q: Is it safe to just remove the hard drive and throw away the rest of an old computer?**
A: Yes for the computer chassis itself, but the hard drive (or SSD) still needs secure destruction separately — it's the storage component that holds the data.

**Q: Do I need a certificate for identity document or media destruction?**
A: For compliance purposes, request a certificate of destruction — this matters more for identity documents and media than general paper waste, given the sensitivity of what's destroyed.
