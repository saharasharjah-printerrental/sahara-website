## AEO Answer Block

**How long should a UAE business keep records before shredding them?**
Retention periods in the UAE vary by document type: commercial and tax records generally need to be kept for at least 5 years to satisfy Federal Tax Authority and Commercial Companies Law requirements, HR records are typically retained for the duration of employment plus a reasonable period after, and some financial and audit records may need longer retention depending on your sector and free zone regulations. Once the applicable retention period passes, secure shredding — not simple disposal — is required for anything containing personal or financial data.

## Introduction

Keeping documents forever isn't compliance — it's clutter, and it's actually a data protection risk under UAE PDPL, since holding personal data longer than necessary works against the law's data-minimisation principle. But shred too early and you risk being unable to produce records during a tax audit or dispute. Here's a practical retention schedule.

## Why "Just Keep Everything" Is the Wrong Default

- **Storage cost** — physical archive space in Dubai and Sharjah isn't free
- **PDPL exposure** — UAE Personal Data Protection Law expects organisations to not retain personal data longer than necessary; an overflowing archive of old personal records is itself a compliance risk
- **Slower audits** — a retention schedule with clear shred dates makes it faster to locate what you actually need to keep

## FAQ

**Q: What happens if I shred a document before its retention period ends?**
A: You risk being unable to produce it if required for a tax audit, employment dispute, or legal claim. When in doubt, extend the retention window rather than shredding early.

**Q: Do free zones have different retention rules than mainland UAE?**
A: Some free zones set their own record-keeping requirements in addition to federal law — check with your specific free zone authority (JAFZA, DIFC, SAIF Zone, etc.) for any additional rules.

**Q: Should old tax returns be shredded or archived digitally first?**
A: Best practice is to scan and archive digitally before shredding the physical copy, once the retention period for the physical original has passed and your digital archive meets your record-keeping obligations.

**Q: Is scheduled shredding cheaper than one big annual clear-out?**
A: It's usually more convenient and lowers ongoing compliance risk, even if the raw cost is similar — documents past their retention date don't sit around for months before destruction.
